# Whole Knowledge: Contextual Observation Protocol

Research stage: Direct behavioral discovery  
Sample: Five target participants  
Session length: 30–45 minutes  
Status: Ready for field use; not authorization to build a V0  
Last updated: 2026-08-25

## Decision this study must inform

This study tests the current problem thesis:

> Serious self-directed language learners need a low-friction way to turn personally encountered language and personally made mistakes into contextual, productive practice that resurfaces for later reuse.

The study is not trying to validate an all-in-one language application. “Language OS” remains a long-term vision, not the V0 scope. The immediate question is whether a repeated, costly breakdown exists between encounter or correction and later productive reuse.

“Serious” is working shorthand for current, goal-directed learning behavior evidenced by authentic-content use and an active personal workflow. It does not mean a minimum daily duration, a streak, a professional goal, or self-identification as a serious learner.

Five sessions can reveal repeated behavior, mechanisms, and strong contradictions inside the deliberately selected target segment. They cannot estimate market size, population frequency, or how common that segment is. Because the screener intentionally requires multiple resources and either a production gap or recurring mistakes, neither condition may later be reported as a discovery.

## Research principles

- Observe recent behavior and real artifacts before asking for explanations.
- Ask participants to operate their own tools. The moderator should not complete tasks for them.
- Distinguish direct observation, timestamped or historical artifact evidence, static-artifact reconstruction, and unverified recollection in the notes.
- Follow one or two real language items through the workflow instead of cataloguing every feature in every tool.
- Record deliberate non-capture as a valid choice, not automatically as a failure.
- Treat a smooth specialist-tool workflow as counter-evidence, not an exception to explain away.
- Do not pitch Whole Knowledge, show a prototype, solicit feature ideas, or ask whether a participant would use or pay for an imagined product.
- Do not infer demand from enthusiasm. Evidence is what people already do, lose, repeat, defer, abandon, or pay to work around.

## Chosen observation method

Use **artifact-led reconstruction with live demonstration**. Each participant traces two anchors where available:

1. **N anchor — newest encounter:** Open the participant’s most recent authentic-content activity and work backward from the stopping point to the first language item they remember noticing, whether they kept it or not. If the source history is unavailable, use their newest captured item and record the selection limitation.
2. **H anchor — historical chain:** Enumerate every tool or physical surface used in the last 30 days that contains dated captures, corrections, highlights, annotations, or bookmarks. In each history, find its newest eligible artifact from 7–30 days ago. Compare those candidates and choose the newest timestamp across all histories. It is eligible whether it was reviewed, reused, ignored, abandoned, or has no later activity.

An eligible H artifact is one discrete target-language word, expression, sentence, or pattern that the learner marked or saved, or one discrete original speaking/writing error with its correction. A raw content-history entry without evidence that the learner noticed a specific language item is not eligible. The minimum evidence is the dated original artifact plus its current state or a dated later event.

Resolve ties in this order: more precise recorded timestamp, then alphabetical normalized tool or surface name, then topmost item in that surface’s visible history. Record the candidate list and tie-break. This deterministic traversal is performed during the session; the participant does not preselect H.

Do not skip an eligible H anchor in favor of a more memorable success or failure. If there is no eligible H anchor, mark the historical chain unevaluable; the session may still inform capture behavior but cannot count toward the causal decision gate. Where safe and practical, the participant repeats a relevant action live.

This method is the best fit for a 30–45 minute session because the full encounter-to-reuse loop may span days. A purely live study session has greater immediacy but is unlikely to reveal resurfacing or recurring corrections. A diary study would provide stronger longitudinal evidence, but it is a possible follow-up rather than a substitute for these first five sessions.

Assign every event an anchor ID and lineage ID. Evidence from N and H must never be stitched into one chain. A transformed descendant may stay in the H lineage only when an artifact or direct observation connects it to the original. The moderator must label reconstructed events as reconstructed. A polished explanation is not equivalent to an observed action. Never infer future resurfacing from where an item is expected to appear; record the outcome as unknown when no historical evidence exists.

## 1. Participant screener

### Recruitment message

> We are studying how experienced, self-directed language learners handle useful language and corrections across the tools they already use. This is a 30–45 minute research session in which you will show a recent real example from your current workflow. We are not testing you, selling a product, or asking you to evaluate a concept. You may hide or skip anything private.

Recruit adults only for this round so consent and recording do not require a separate guardian process. Compensate participants at a fair local research rate whether or not their workflow supports the thesis.

### Screener questions

Ask in this order. Do not reveal the desired answers.

1. Which language or languages are you currently learning, and for how long?
2. How would you describe what you can currently do in your main target language? Ask for a recent example, not only a level label.
3. In the last 30 days, what have you read, watched, or listened to that was created for native or proficient users rather than primarily for learners?
4. Which resources, people, or tools have you used for this language in the last 30 days? What did you use each one for?
5. Tell me about the last time you noticed a word or expression you understood when reading or listening but could not readily use when speaking or writing. What happened?
6. Tell me about the last correction you received, or a mistake you noticed more than once. What happened after the correction?
7. In the last seven days, did you save any language from something you read, watched, heard, wrote, or said? If yes, where is it now?
8. Would you be comfortable sharing your screen or device and showing one recent, non-sensitive example using your existing tools? You may redact or skip private material.
9. Are you employed by, researching, or currently building a language-learning product? If yes, record it for sampling and conflict review.

### Eligibility gate

A participant qualifies only when all required conditions are met:

| Condition | Required behavioral evidence |
|---|---|
| Intermediate or advanced | Can engage with some authentic content without the session depending entirely on beginner course material. A self-reported CEFR label alone is insufficient. |
| Self-directed | Personally chooses at least part of the resources, routine, or practice outside a fixed class curriculum. |
| Multiple resources | Used two or more materially different resources, tools, or people in the last 30 days. |
| Authentic content | Can name a concrete example used in the last 30 days. |
| Relevant tension | Can recount a recent recognition-to-production gap, a recurring corrected mistake, or both. |
| Observable workflow | Can show at least one recent non-sensitive artifact or tool path during the session. |

Exclude a candidate from this round if their only current activity is a beginner course, they cannot show or reconstruct a recent workflow, or their answers concern a workflow they used only in the distant past. A learner who uses a specialized system successfully remains eligible and is valuable counter-evidence.

These criteria intentionally enrich the sample for the user profile named in the current thesis. They do **not** allow the study to claim that multi-tool use, production gaps, or recurring corrections are prevalent. The study may only test whether the hypothesized connective breakdown actually causes cost or failed reuse for people already in this segment.

Maintain a disposition log for everyone screened:

| Candidate ID | Eligible? | Reason included or excluded | Target tension recorded | Resource count | Artifact available? | Session completed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### Five-seat sampling plan

Do not select five near-identical Anki power users. Fill the sample deliberately:

- Two participants whose clearest tension is recognition-to-production.
- Two whose clearest tension is recurring speaking or writing corrections.
- One who shows both, or whichever pattern is underrepresented after recruitment.
- At least two who receive correction from a tutor, teacher, exchange partner, or proficient speaker.
- At least two who use flashcards or spaced review.
- At least two who use notes, a spreadsheet, a document, a journal, or a personal knowledge tool.
- At least two different primary target languages and at least two different main tool ecosystems.
- Include both desktop-heavy and phone-heavy workflows if recruitment allows.

This is contrast sampling, not a demographic quota or a representative sample.

## Session preparation and consent

### Message sent 24 hours before the session

Ask the participant to have the following available without cleaning or reorganizing it for the study:

- the tools they currently use;
- access to their recent authentic-content, capture, and correction history without preselecting a “good” example;
- the place where captured items or corrections are reviewed, if one exists;
- any history needed to trace an item from 7–30 days ago, if such history exists.

Tell them not to expose passwords, private chats, names, payment details, or unrelated personal content. The participant controls the device and may pause, redact, or skip any item.

### Opening consent script

> Thank you for showing me how you already learn. I am studying the workflow, not evaluating your language ability. I will ask you to show a recent real example and may ask what happened before or after it. There is no product demo. Please hide or skip anything private. You can pause or stop at any time. Is it okay for me to take notes? Is it okay to record the screen or audio for research analysis?

Record note consent and recording consent separately. If recording is declined, continue with timestamped notes. Assign participant IDs P01–P05 and keep identifying recruitment data separate from research notes. Decide and disclose the raw-recording deletion date before recruitment; a reasonable default is deletion within 30 days after synthesis.

## 2. Observation protocol

The timings are guides. Follow the real workflow when it exposes useful evidence.

### 0:00–0:04 — Consent and present context

- Read the opening script and confirm permissions.
- Ask what language activity the participant most recently completed and which devices are involved today.
- Restate that there is no correct workflow and that rough or unfinished artifacts are useful.

Evidence sought: current context, devices available, and whether the participant changes behavior because they feel observed.

### 0:04–0:08 — Select recent artifacts

- Select N and H using the ordered search procedure above.
- Record the anchor ID, lineage ID, original date, selection path, and source before discussing what happened.
- If no later outcome can be verified for H, record “unknown” rather than substituting an expected workflow. Do not count that chain as positive or negative.

Evidence sought: recency, provenance, and an artifact from which the workflow can be traced.

### 0:08–0:20 — Trace encounter, capture, and enrichment

Have the participant start at the original source where possible and reconstruct the event in order.

Observe:

- what first drew attention to the item;
- what they did before deciding to save or ignore it;
- every tool, screen, device, person, copy, lookup, reformat, and classification step;
- what source context was preserved or discarded;
- time spent making the item “reviewable”;
- any duplicate entry, interruption, search, or cleanup;
- a nearby item they intentionally ignored, if one exists.

If safe, ask them to capture a comparable item live. Mark this as a study-elicited task rather than ordinary behavior.

### 0:20–0:31 — Trace review, production, and correction

Ask whether the historical item appeared again and request visible evidence. Do not ask where it “would” appear and do not assume it did.

Observe:

- whether it entered a queue automatically, manually, or not at all;
- how the next review is selected;
- whether the task requires recognition, cued recall, constrained production, or free production;
- whether original context is visible during review;
- whether the participant must speak, type, write, or only judge familiarity;
- what happens when the answer is incomplete or wrong;
- whether the result changes future practice.

For the correction thread, trace the original error, the corrected form, the explanation or source, storage, later review, and any subsequent reuse. Ask for visible evidence of resurfacing rather than accepting “I usually review it.” Record “unknown” if the correction is too recent or the evidence is unavailable.

### 0:31–0:38 — Daily choice and abandonment

Ask the participant to show how they decided what to practice today or during their last study session.

Observe:

- prompts, queues, calendars, lists, tutor assignments, habits, or improvisation;
- time and uncertainty before starting meaningful practice;
- work selected because it is easy to resume versus work avoided despite importance;
- what happened after the last visible step, including deferral or abandonment if it occurred;
- the exact point at which curation, review, or reuse ended, without presuming that ending was a failure.

Do not treat a flexible or intuitive routine as broken unless the participant demonstrates a cost or failed goal.

### 0:38–0:43 — Factual debrief

- Replay the observed sequence in neutral terms and ask what is missing or out of order.
- Verify which steps were directly observed, reconstructed from artifacts, or recalled.
- Confirm approximate frequency and time only after the behavior has been shown.
- Ask about the most recent exception when the workflow went differently or worked unusually well.

### 0:43–0:45 — Close

- Ask permission for one follow-up clarification if synthesis reveals an ambiguous fact.
- Thank the participant and stop recording before any informal conversation.
- Do not reveal or pitch a product concept after the session; it can contaminate later referrals and follow-ups.

### Thirty-minute compression

For a 30-minute session, keep consent and artifact selection to five minutes, then trace one historical item with a known outcome for 20 minutes and reserve five minutes for verification. Capture behavior for the newest item, daily choice, or correction handling may be omitted. Do not trade away one coherent causal chain in order to touch every research question.

## 3. Neutral moderator questions

Use these as probes, not a questionnaire to read end to end.

### Recent behavior

- “Please show me the most recent time this happened.”
- “Where did this begin?”
- “What happened next?”
- “What were you looking at when you made that choice?”
- “Can you show me the artifact from that step?”
- “Was that step what happened then, or what you would normally do?”

### Capture and ignore decisions

- “What did you notice here?”
- “What did you do with it?”
- “What told you to keep this one?”
- “What happened to the items around it?”
- “What happened with the item immediately before or after this one?”

### Handoffs and context

- “I saw you move from this screen to that one. What moved with you?”
- “What did you have to add, remove, or rewrite?”
- “Where could you find the original source later?”
- “What information was available there but is not here?”
- “What, if anything, happened after it arrived here?”

### Review and production

- “Did this item appear again? What evidence can we see?”
- “Please show me what you have to do when it appears.”
- “What can you see before answering?”
- “What counts as a successful answer here?”
- “What happens after an answer you consider wrong or incomplete?”
- “Show me the last time you used a reviewed item in your own speaking or writing, if you can.”

### Corrections and recurring mistakes

- “Please show me the original attempt and what changed.”
- “Who or what supplied the correction?”
- “What did you do immediately after receiving it?”
- “Where is that correction now?”
- “Did it appear again? If so, what led to that?”
- “Please show me another occurrence of the same issue, if one exists.”

### Daily choice and abandonment

- “How did your last study session begin?”
- “What was the first decision you made?”
- “What did you choose not to do?”
- “Please show me something you intended to return to but did not.”
- “What happened after this point?”
- “What happened on a day when this workflow went smoothly?”

### Frequency and cost, asked only after observation

- “Thinking about the last seven days, on how many days did this happen?”
- “About how long did this example take from noticing it to saving it?”
- “Which part required the most attention?”
- “Did this step require a paid tool or person? What role did that purchase play?”

Avoid “Would you use…?”, “Wouldn’t it be easier if…?”, “Do you want everything in one app?”, and “What feature should we build?” Do not ask “why” when “what happened” or “what were you trying to do” can elicit less rationalized evidence.

### Counting rules and example

- Count one handoff when responsibility for preserving the item, context, or intention crosses a defined boundary. Do not count ordinary navigation within one tool unless the participant reconstructs missing information.
- Count one manual transfer for each discrete copy, retype, import, export, screenshot, or physical transcription action.
- Count duplicate entry only when substantially the same information is entered again because systems do not share it; intentional reformulation for learning is not duplication.
- Count context as lost only when evidence shows it existed before the handoff and is unavailable afterward. Otherwise use “not captured” or “unavailable.”
- Keep participant-estimated historical duration separate from recorded task time.

Example: a participant copies a subtitle sentence into a note, then later retypes a shortened version into a card. Code two handoffs and two manual transfers. Mark the subtitle timing “lost” only if it was visible in the source and absent from both destinations. Mark shortening as “transformed,” not lost, and record whether that transformation helps or harms the later task.

## 5. Friction and handoff measurement framework

### Definitions

A **handoff** occurs when an item, its context, or the learner’s intention crosses between tools, people, devices, screens with separate data models, or physical and digital media. Moving between two screens in one tool counts only if the participant must manually reconstruct context or intent.

**Curation time** is active work required to make encountered language or a correction usable later. It includes lookup, copying, retyping, formatting, tagging, prompt creation, classification, and validation. It excludes the original reading, listening, conversation, or writing activity.

**Resurfacing** requires the same item, mistake pattern, or intentionally transformed descendant to become available for later practice. A note that remains searchable but never appears or is deliberately retrieved has not automatically resurfaced.

**Productive practice** requires the learner to generate target-language output before seeing the full answer. Recognition and self-rating familiarity do not qualify.

### Separate measurement dimensions

Do not combine friction into one ordinal score. For every relevant event, record these independently:

| Dimension | Values or measurement |
|---|---|
| Time | Recorded seconds, artifact-derived interval, or participant-estimated range |
| Manual work | Number and type of copy, re-entry, lookup, formatting, classification, or validation steps |
| Interruption | No interruption, observed pause, search, device change, or return on another occasion |
| Context state | Preserved, transformed, not captured, unavailable, or demonstrably lost |
| Outcome | Completed, deferred, abandoned, duplicated, or unknown |
| Recurrence | Artifact count, dated examples, or participant estimate with confidence |
| Downstream consequence | None shown, unclear, or demonstrated effect on the participant’s stated goal |
| Participant assessment | Their description after the behavior is captured, clearly separated from moderator interpretation |

More tools, handoffs, time, or transformations are not inherently worse. A repeated five-second interruption may matter more than a valued two-minute reformulation. Severity comes from the demonstrated consequence and recurrence, not the moderator’s aesthetic preference for fewer steps.

### Handoff dimensions

For each handoff, record:

1. **Initiation cost:** taps, clicks, searches, logins, device changes, or setup before transfer.
2. **Transformation cost:** copying, retyping, translation, reformulation, card construction, tagging, or validation.
3. **Context integrity:** which of source, surrounding language, audio, situation, intended meaning, original attempt, and correction provenance survive.
4. **Queue integrity:** whether the item reaches a real review or practice queue, remains only searchable, or becomes orphaned.
5. **Practice integrity:** whether the later task matches the learner’s intended use, especially recognition versus production.
6. **Feedback closure:** whether failure or correction changes a future prompt or practice decision.
7. **Participant response:** record literal hesitation, pause, apology, or change of action, then ask what was happening. Never infer frustration or relief from behavior alone.

### Core session measures

- Number of unique surfaces involved in one traced loop.
- Number of handoffs and manual transfers per traced item.
- Active curation time and decision time, reported as observed value or participant estimate.
- Number and type of context fields lost at each handoff.
- Whether the captured item enters review, and how.
- Practice task types reached and whether each fits the participant’s demonstrated goal; do not assume free production is universally superior.
- Artifact-verified resurfacing as `n/N`, with the selection basis and evidence class for every correction in the denominator.
- Deferred, abandoned, duplicated, and demonstrated goal-impact events, each with its evidence reference.
- Existing workaround effort: recurring minutes per week, duplicate systems, paid assistance, or deliberately reduced capture.

Do not collapse these measures into one impressive-looking score. With five participants, report counts, ranges, and the causal sequence around each severe event.

## 6. Synthesis framework

### After each session

Within 24 hours:

1. Correct timestamps and separate O, A1, A2, and S evidence.
2. Draw the participant’s actual path: encounter → decision → capture → preparation → review → production → correction → reuse, leaving missing stages blank.
3. Mark each tool or person boundary, deferral, abandonment, and demonstrated goal consequence.
4. Attempt to identify a success case, failure case, and contradiction. Record “none evidenced” rather than manufacturing one.
5. Record unanswered facts without filling gaps through inference.

### Coding definitions fixed before session one

A **qualifying causal chain** contains all of the following for one H lineage. Every element must be supported by O or A1 evidence; A2 and S may add context but cannot establish a critical link:

1. a personally encountered language item or personally made mistake;
2. at least one real handoff or preparation step;
3. a defined exposure: repeated manual preparation, context change, or queue/practice mismatch;
4. a downstream path failure distinct from that exposure: the anchor misses an intended practice occasion, reaches the wrong task type, or does not resurface for later reuse; and
5. a separate demonstrated goal impact such as the item being unavailable when needed, the same error recurring, relevant practice being displaced or abandoned, or a recurring workaround consuming time or money. Time counts as impact only when recurrence and burden or displacement are evidenced.

Freeze this mechanism codebook before session one:

| Code | Exposure | Path failure | Candidate goal impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 Context break | Source, intended meaning, original attempt, or correction provenance changes or disappears during a handoff | Later practice cannot reconstruct the intended use | Wrong use, avoidable relearning, or item abandoned |
| M2 Recognition trap | Saved material enters recognition or familiarity review | It never reaches a task matching a demonstrated productive goal | Item remains unavailable in speaking/writing or requires a separate workaround |
| M3 Correction orphan | A personal error is corrected or stored | No later goal-matched practice path exists | Same error recurs or correction must be manually rediscovered |
| M4 Curation displacement | Preparing or transferring personal material repeatedly consumes attention | Capture, review preparation, or actual practice is deferred or abandoned | Less goal-relevant practice occurs or a recurring paid/manual bridge is maintained |

Assign exactly one primary path-failure code and one primary goal-impact code to each candidate chain:

| Path code | Fixed assignment rule |
|---|---|
| P1 Context mismatch | At a later task, context required for the participant’s demonstrated use is absent or materially changed, and O/A1 evidence shows the task cannot support that use. |
| P2 Task mismatch | The H lineage reaches review, but the task requires recognition when the demonstrated goal requires learner-generated output before the answer is shown. |
| P3 Queue omission | By the end of the original capture/correction occasion, the H lineage has no defined route into later practice and remains only stored or searchable. A goal impact is still required. |
| P4 Opportunity miss | After H, at least one verified queue due date or goal-matched practice occasion occurs and the lineage does not appear. |
| P5 Curation deferral | Preparation is deferred beyond the original study occasion or abandoned, as shown by the dated state or history. |

| Impact code | Fixed assignment rule |
|---|---|
| I1 Failed later use | A dated later speaking/writing artifact or task shows the item unavailable, misused, or avoided. |
| I2 Repeated error | A dated later artifact shows the same correction pattern recurring. |
| I3 Practice displaced | A planned or begun goal-matched practice activity is deferred, reduced, or abandoned, with O/A1 evidence. |
| I4 Recurring bridge burden | The same manual bridge appears on at least two dated occasions, or a paid service/person is used specifically to perform it. |
| I5 Re-created knowledge | A dated event shows the learner re-finding or re-creating material because the earlier artifact or context was unavailable. |

A **goal-matched practice occasion** is a dated speaking, writing, tutor, or review event that requires the kind of output evidenced by the participant’s current activity or external obligation. A stated aspiration alone is insufficient. **Later reuse** means the learner generates the same item or correction pattern in target-language output after H and before seeing the full answer or correction.

Absence may be coded only inside the H observation window, from its timestamp through the session date, and only when at least one verified due event or goal-matched practice occasion creates a fair opportunity. Otherwise assign unknown, not P4. P3 may describe the original queue state without such an occasion, but it cannot become a positive chain without a separately evidenced I1–I5 impact.

Daily practice selection remains an observed secondary question. It counts toward the causal thesis only when it can be linked to one of M1–M4 for the same H lineage.

Two cases show the **same causal chain** only when M, P, and I codes match, even if the language or software differs. New mechanisms discovered during synthesis are exploratory and cannot satisfy the 3/5 decision gate in this round; they require a follow-up study. Log every codebook or case-assignment change as a protocol deviation.

Two researchers independently code every H lineage as positive, countercase, or unknown and assign M/P/I codes without seeing each other’s labels. Compare codes only after all five first-pass codings are complete. Resolve a disagreement by returning to the timestamped event rows and artifacts; if the frozen rules still do not produce agreement, classify the case as unknown. Never resolve a disagreement by majority intuition or by weakening an evidence requirement.

A **tool ecosystem** is defined by the primary storage-and-review mechanism, not by brand count. For example, an Anki-centered workflow and a notes-plus-tutor workflow are different ecosystems; two differently named flashcard applications with the same path may not provide meaningful contrast. Freeze these definitions before session one and log any later change as a protocol deviation.

### Case-pattern rules

- **Recurring case pattern:** supported by O, A1, or A2 evidence in at least two participants.
- **Cross-ecosystem case pattern:** observed in at least three participants across at least two different tool ecosystems.
- **Isolated signal:** one participant only; retain it but do not use it to define V0.
- **Stated preference:** report separately and never promote it to a behavioral theme without evidence.
- **Counter-pattern:** an existing workflow succeeds with low friction, a learner deliberately rejects resurfacing, or specialist separation is valuable. Give counter-patterns equal visibility.

For each theme, write:

1. the triggering situation;
2. the observed behavior;
3. the breakdown or successful workaround;
4. its time, money, attention, or learning consequence;
5. evidence IDs and evidence class;
6. how many participants and ecosystems show it;
7. at least one plausible rival explanation and the evidence for or against it;
8. protocol deviations, unknowns, and the strongest counter-evidence;
9. the current thesis effect: support, narrow, contradict, or unrelated;
10. the smallest assumption that should be tested next without building software.

### Final synthesis outputs

- Five individual workflow maps.
- One cross-participant matrix.
- Ranked severe handoffs and abandonment points.
- A context-loss inventory tied to demonstrated consequences.
- A recognition-versus-production map for current review systems.
- A correction-resurfacing map.
- A contradiction log.
- A revised one-sentence problem thesis and first-user definition.
- A decision: stop, revise the thesis, run a follow-up study, or proceed to a manual V0 test.

## 7. Criteria that would falsify the current thesis

Classify every H chain before applying a decision:

| Case classification | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Positive | O/A1 evidence meets every element of one predefined qualifying causal chain for the same H lineage. |
| Countercase | O/A1 evidence shows the relevant H lineage reached goal-matched productive practice or later reuse without P1–P5; or deliberate non-capture served the demonstrated goal in a dated later occasion. |
| Unknown | The chain or outcome is unevaluable, relies on self-report for a critical link, or does not provide a fair opportunity for the mechanism to occur. |

Apply this symmetric decision rule:

| Result | Decision |
|---|---|
| At least 3 positive cases with the same predefined mechanism | Eligible for the behavioral evidence gate below. |
| At least 3 artifact-supported countercases relevant to the same proposed mechanism | Reject or materially narrow that mechanism. Do not select a V0 from it. |
| Any other mix, including 0–1 positives without 3 countercases | Inconclusive. Recruit a follow-up contrast sample; do not select a V0. |

The current thesis is also materially contradicted when:

1. at least three participants’ O/A1-supported workflows already move relevant items or corrections into goal-matched productive practice and later reuse without P1–P5;
2. at least three intentionally use capture only as temporary comprehension support, with observed goals and later behavior that do not require resurfacing;
3. the demonstrated consequence in at least four cases is better explained by feedback quality, lack of practice time, insufficient conversation access, or another rival cause rather than the measured handoff; or
4. the only repeated pattern is “uses multiple tools” without a repeated mechanism connecting those handoffs to failed productive reuse.

### Sub-theses to falsify independently

A case is **applicable** only when its H lineage contains the relevant opportunity and the critical events are evaluable through O or A1 evidence. Each sub-thesis requires at least three applicable cases; otherwise its result is inconclusive.

- **Capture:** In at least three applicable cases, encountered language is used immediately or captured with sufficient context without a demonstrated costly workaround.
- **Production:** In at least three applicable cases, the recognition-to-production gap is not connected to captured language, or current productive practice already addresses it.
- **Mistakes:** In at least three applicable cases, corrections reliably reach later goal-matched practice through the existing workflow, or recurring mistakes have a different demonstrated cause.
- **Daily choice:** In at least three applicable cases, participants begin useful practice without consequential delay or abandonment; queues or routines already make the next action clear.
- **Resurfacing:** In at least three applicable cases, deliberate retrieval or search is sufficient for the demonstrated goal; the study provides no evidence that unprompted resurfacing is missing.

Do not rescue a failed thesis by broadening the product back into “Language OS.” A failure should reduce scope, redirect the problem, or stop the project before feature work.

## 8. Criteria that would justify proceeding to V0

Passing this section authorizes only a manual, narrow V0 test. It does not authorize application feature implementation.

### Behavioral evidence gate

All conditions must pass:

1. All five completed sessions meet the intended target profile; any replacement and its reason appear in the disposition log.
2. At least three of five show the same predefined qualifying causal chain with no critical link supported only by recollection.
3. The chain appears in at least two different primary storage-and-review ecosystems.
4. At least two show recurrence through separate dated artifacts or occasions, rather than estimating frequency from memory alone.
5. The relevant practice task and later reuse match a demonstrated participant goal; the opportunity is not merely cleaner storage, fewer tools, or prettier tracking.
6. Successful countercases bound a narrower first-user segment instead of requiring them to be dismissed as exceptions.

Fewer than three positive cases is inconclusive unless at least three artifact-supported countercases reject the same mechanism. Severity in one or two cases cannot override this rule.

### Manual-test feasibility gate

All conditions must also pass before running a concierge-style V0 test:

1. The evidence supports one bounded user, recurrent trigger, core job, and measurable behavioral outcome without replacing specialist tools.
2. A targeted check of the participants’ current configurations finds no known simple remedy that removes the observed causal chain. If a configuration, training step, or integration appears sufficient, test that first.
3. The team can perform the proposed connective work manually with new participant-supplied material, without application feature code.
4. Success is defined before the test as repeat use with new personal material plus artifact-verified, goal-matched productive resurfacing or reuse. Satisfaction and stated willingness do not count.
5. The manual test can reveal added administration or harm to an intentionally separated workflow; that question belongs in the intervention test, not this observation study.

### Minimum decision statement after five sessions

Complete this without product language:

> When [specific first user] encounters or produces [specific artifact] during [specific real activity], they currently [observed workaround]. Because [repeated handoff or loss], [demonstrated consequence] occurs. We observed this in [count]/5 participants across [count] tool ecosystems. The strongest counter-evidence was [counter-pattern]. Our next no-code test is [manual test], and success means [behavioral outcome].

If this statement cannot be completed without relying on self-report for the critical causal links, do not select a V0 yet.

## Moderator quality checklist

Before each session:

- [ ] Participant passed every required screener condition.
- [ ] Participant was asked to bring recent, unpolished artifacts.
- [ ] Consent, privacy, compensation, and recording terms are clear.
- [ ] No prototype, feature list, or product pitch is present.

During each session:

- [ ] Start from a real recent event.
- [ ] Ask “show me” before “tell me.”
- [ ] Trace one item across tools rather than touring features.
- [ ] Capture timestamps, handoffs, context loss, and moderator interventions.
- [ ] Label observed, reconstructed, and stated evidence separately.
- [ ] Seek a successful workflow and follow the selected historical item to whatever outcome actually occurred.
- [ ] Do not imply that capture, resurfacing, or one integrated app is desirable.

After each session:

- [ ] Record the strongest thesis support and strongest contradiction.
- [ ] Preserve raw counts rather than inventing a composite score.
- [ ] Remove unrelated personal data from notes.
- [ ] Update the cross-participant matrix before the next session.
